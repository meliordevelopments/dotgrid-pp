DotGrid Privacy Policy

Last updated 24 September 2026

In short: DotGrid is a local-first bullet journal. Everything you write stays on your device by default. There are no accounts, no servers, no ads and no analytics. You can optionally back up your journal to a folder you choose or to your own Dropbox — the Dropbox backup is encrypted with your own passphrase before it leaves your device. We never receive or see your journal.

This policy explains how the DotGrid app for Android ("the app") handles information.

What the app stores, and where

Everything you enter — tasks, notes, events, collections, tags, links between entries, any URLs you attach, and your preferences — is stored in a private database on your device that only DotGrid can read. It is not sent anywhere by default.

We do not operate a server that stores, sees, or has access to your journal. If you uninstall the app or clear its data, your journal is removed from the device unless you have backed it up or exported it.

Backup: local folder and Dropbox (both optional)

DotGrid offers two backup destinations you can turn on in the app's backup settings. Neither is required to use the app, and both are things you start — DotGrid never backs up or restores without your action.

Local folder

You can choose a folder on your device (or on removable storage) using Android's own folder picker, and DotGrid will save backup files there — daily and on demand — keeping the most recent copies and removing older ones. This file never leaves your device through the app; it stays in the folder you chose, in plain (unencrypted) JSON, so you can always open or move it yourself.

Dropbox

If you connect Dropbox, DotGrid can back up your journal to your own Dropbox account — daily and whenever you ask.

  • Encrypted before it leaves. Each Dropbox backup is encrypted on your device with a key derived from a passphrase only you know. We could not read it even if we had it, and we never do.
  • Narrowest access available. DotGrid uses Dropbox's App folder access, a single sandboxed folder for DotGrid — never the rest of your Dropbox.
  • Direct connection. Your device talks to Dropbox directly over an encrypted connection. Nothing passes through any server of ours.
  • Your password stays with Dropbox. You sign in on Dropbox's own screen and never share your password with DotGrid. The app receives and stores a refresh token — encrypted with a key that is generated on your device and never leaves it — and uses that token to request short-lived access to your Dropbox as needed. Disconnecting Dropbox in the app deletes that token from your device immediately; you can also revoke access from your Dropbox account settings.
  • Backup, not sync. Restoring from a backup only happens when you start it, and replaces your journal only after you confirm.

Backup files stored in your Dropbox account are also subject to Dropbox's Privacy Policy.

DotGrid does not currently offer Google Drive backup. If that changes in a future version, this policy will be updated before the feature ships.

Calendar

If you grant calendar permission, DotGrid reads events from your device's calendars so it can show them alongside your journal, and creates one calendar of its own on your device for events you create in the app. It never edits or deletes events in any calendar it does not own. Calendar data is not sent anywhere by the app.

Reminders and notifications

If you allow notifications, DotGrid uses them to show reminders you set. Reminders are scheduled and delivered on your device.

Biometric lock (optional)

If you turn on the app lock, unlocking is handled by your device's own fingerprint, face, or PIN system. The app never sees or stores biometric data — it only receives a yes-or-no result from Android.

Export, import and sharing

You can export your journal as JSON or Markdown and import it from JSON. The app reads or writes only the files and locations you pick. Exported files are unencrypted and fully under your control once saved.

When you share text or a link into DotGrid from another app, only what you share is saved, and it stays on your device like any other entry.

Home screen widget

The widget shows your journal content on your home screen. It reads from the same on-device database and sends nothing anywhere. Anyone who can see your home screen can see what the widget displays.

What we don't do

  • No account or sign-in with us — the app does not know who you are.
  • No analytics, advertising, tracking, or crash-reporting services.
  • No selling, renting, or sharing of your information with anyone.
  • No collection of your location, contacts, or device identifiers.

If the app crashes, any log is written only to your device. It is shared only if you choose to send that file yourself.

Permissions

  • Internet — used only to reach Dropbox when you have connected it for backup.
  • Calendar — to read device events and manage DotGrid's own calendar, as described above.
  • Notifications — to show reminders you set.
  • Biometric — to unlock the app, if you turn the lock on.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your journal stays on your device until you delete it. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data in Android settings removes it from the device. A local-folder backup remains in the folder you chose, and a Dropbox backup remains in your Dropbox, until you delete it yourself. Because we hold none of your data, there is nothing for us to delete on our side.

Deleting an entry inside the app removes it from your live journal immediately, but a copy of it can remain inside backups already made until those backups age out. DotGrid keeps the 14 most recent backups per destination and removes older ones automatically as new ones are made — so a deleted entry does not disappear from every backup the moment you delete it, only once the backups holding it have rotated out.

Children

DotGrid is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal data from anyone, since it does not collect personal data from any user.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes — most likely because a new feature connects to a new service — the "Last updated" date above will change, and material changes will be noted in the app's release notes.
